Calibrate this laptop

Every laptop hears the ESP differently, so each one needs its own numbers. Put the ESP at a measured distance from this laptop (tape measure, same height as the laptop, nobody in between), keep it still, and press Measure. Do it at 2–3 distances, e.g. 0.3 m, 1 m and 3 m. Measuring the same distance again replaces the old reading.

Position is approximate (Bluetooth signal strength, no UWB): expect ~0.5-1.5 m error. Each laptop turns the signal into a distance with RSSI = A − 10·n·log10(d). The 3 closest laptops count most, then a smoothing filter (Kalman) steadies the dot.

Laptops

Other laptops join by running python tools/reporter.py --server http://<this-ip>:8000 --laptop <name>. Set touch: hold the tag against that laptop, then press its button (uses the last 2 s).

Calibrate room (noise floor)

Hold the tag still at the crosshair (pick a spot on the map, or leave it to use the centre of the laptops) and press the button. Every laptop records the signal. Because we know the real distance to the spot, the standard formula RSSI = A − 10·n·log10(d) tells us each laptop's true 1 m level (A), and the wobble of the signal is its noise floor.
